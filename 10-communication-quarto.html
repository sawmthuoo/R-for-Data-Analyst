1 Introduction

This report presents a descriptive analysis of credit card holder data drawn from the 2026 quarterly credit report. The dataset captures financial and demographic characteristics of 400 cardholders, including income, credit limit, credit rating, outstanding balance, and personal background.

The purpose of this report is to describe the profile of cardholders, understand the distribution of credit balances, and identify the financial variables most associated with higher credit card balances. The findings are intended to support informed decision-making for credit management and portfolio review.

2 Background

Credit card balance — the outstanding amount owed on a credit card — is a key indicator of credit utilization and financial exposure for both cardholders and lenders. Understanding which factors are associated with higher balances helps identify patterns in borrowing behavior and informs risk assessment.

Several financial variables are expected to play a role. Credit limit and credit rating are direct measures of a cardholder’s credit standing and are likely to be closely linked to balance levels. Income, while not directly controlling credit issuance, reflects a cardholder’s repayment capacity and may be moderately associated with balance. Demographic characteristics — including age, education, gender, ethnicity, and student or marital status — provide context for understanding the diversity of the cardholder population.

This analysis focuses on describing these variables and examining their relationship to credit card balance.

3 Method

3.3 Data preparation

The raw CSV file was imported using the readr package. The unnamed row-index column was removed, column names were standardized to lowercase snake_case using the janitor package, and empty rows and columns were dropped. No missing values were identified in any column following this cleaning step.

3.4 Analytical approach

The analysis proceeds in two stages. First, a demographic profile of the cardholder population is constructed using frequency counts and percentages for categorical variables, and summary statistics (mean, median, standard deviation) for numeric variables. Second, the key analysis examines the distribution of credit card balance and its association with financial and demographic variables using summary tables and visualizations.

All analyses were conducted in R using the tidyverse suite of packages. Visualizations were produced with ggplot2.

4.2 Key analysis

4.2.1 Distribution of income

Figure 2 shows the distribution of annual income. The distribution is strongly right-skewed, with the majority of cardholders earning below $60,000 annually and a long tail extending toward higher earners.

4.2.2 Distribution of credit card balance

Figure 3 shows the distribution of credit card balance. A notable number of cardholders carry a zero balance — these are cardholders who pay off their balance in full each month. Among those who do carry a balance, the distribution is broadly spread, with some cardholders approaching the upper limit of $2,000.

4.2.3 Relationship between financial variables and balance

Credit limit and credit rating are the variables most strongly associated with credit card balance (Figure 4). The correlation between limit and balance is 0.862, and between rating and balance is 0.864, both indicating a strong positive relationship. Cardholders with higher credit limits and ratings tend to carry higher balances.

Income shows a moderate positive association with balance (r = 0.464). As seen in Figure 5, the relationship between income and balance is weaker than that of credit limit, and is consistent across ethnic groups.

5 Conclusion and discussion

This report provides a descriptive profile of 400 credit card holders based on the 2026 quarterly credit data. The key findings are as follows.

The cardholder population is demographically diverse, with Caucasian cardholders forming a plurality (49.8%) alongside Asian (25.5%) and African American (24.8%) groups. The sample is approximately gender-balanced, predominantly non-student, and mostly married.

Income in this population is right-skewed, with a median of $33.1K and a mean pulled upward to $45.2K by a smaller number of high earners. This skewness is an important consideration when summarizing this variable: the median provides a more representative picture of typical income than the mean.

The strongest predictor of credit card balance in this dataset is credit limit (r = 0.862), followed closely by credit rating (r = 0.864). Both reflect the lender’s assessment of a cardholder’s creditworthiness and directly shape the upper bound of borrowing. Income has a moderate association with balance (r = 0.464), indicating that higher earners tend to carry larger balances, though the relationship is less direct.

Across ethnic groups and genders, average balances are similar — the data do not show meaningful differences by these demographic categories alone. The most notable demographic contrast is by student status: student cardholders carry substantially higher average balances than their non-student counterparts, which may reflect different spending needs or repayment behaviors and warrants closer monitoring.

This analysis is descriptive in nature. Observed associations — particularly between credit limit and balance — should not be interpreted as causal without further investigation. Future analysis could examine multivariate models to isolate the independent contribution of each variable, and could investigate the subset of cardholders with a zero balance separately from those who actively carry debt.
